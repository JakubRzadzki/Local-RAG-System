The Solar System: quick facts

The Sun contains about 99.86% of the total mass of the Solar System. Light from the
Sun needs roughly 8 minutes and 20 seconds to reach Earth, which orbits at an average
distance of 149.6 million kilometres (1 astronomical unit).

The four inner planets - Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars - are rocky. Mercury is the
smallest planet and has the shortest year: it circles the Sun in just 88 Earth days.
Venus is the hottest planet, with a surface temperature of about 465 degrees Celsius
because of its thick carbon dioxide atmosphere, and it rotates backwards compared to
most other planets. Mars has the tallest volcano in the Solar System, Olympus Mons,
which rises about 22 kilometres above the surrounding plains.

The outer planets are the gas giants Jupiter and Saturn and the ice giants Uranus and
Neptune. Jupiter is the largest planet; its mass is more than twice that of all the
other planets combined, and its Great Red Spot is a storm that has been observed for
centuries. Saturn is famous for its rings made mostly of water ice. Uranus rotates on
its side with an axial tilt of about 98 degrees. Neptune has the strongest winds of
any planet, reaching more than 2,000 kilometres per hour.

Between Mars and Jupiter lies the asteroid belt, home to the dwarf planet Ceres.
Beyond Neptune, the Kuiper Belt contains Pluto, which was reclassified as a dwarf
planet by the International Astronomical Union in 2006.
